Optics and analog signals

Personal build

Laser vibrometry microphone

Finished, based on a design guide by @1nska

An optical microphone that picks up sound without being near it. Sound makes a window pane vibrate by a few nanometers, and an infrared laser bounced off the glass carries those tiny vibrations back to a receiver.

The transmitter is an 808 nm laser diode driven by an LM317 set up as a current regulator, which holds the laser at a steady current of about 266 mA. The receiver starts with a BPW34 photodiode feeding one TL074 quad op-amp chip. The first stage is a transimpedance amplifier that turns the faint photocurrent into a voltage (1 V per microamp), the second doubles that signal, and the third is a two-step low-pass filter with a buffer that strips out high-frequency noise. The last stage is an LM567 tone decoder, whose output goes low when it detects the signal.

The op-amps run from a ±9 V split supply made from two 9 V batteries, and a 7805 regulator turns that into 5 V for the laser driver and the tone decoder.

  • LM317 current regulator
  • BPW34 photodiode
  • TL074 op-amps
  • Transimpedance amplifier
  • Active filters
  • LM567 tone decoder
  • Split power supply
